# ADR 0016 step 3: grammar synthesis parity against the hand-authored fixtures

- Date opened: 2026-08-16
- ADR: [0016](../decisions/0016-articulate-visual-grammar-and-evaluate-renders-against-it.md), step 3
- Status: **Cycles 1 and 2 judged. Stability check pre-registered, runs not yet captured. Step 3 is not complete.**
- Instructions written against commit: `6cb4ffe0a09465d25e96c198fe6ef107ecbe0735`
- Gate authority: the owner. This document produces evidence and a recommendation. It does not rule.

---

## What is being tested

Step 1 proved that a hand-authored visual grammar can reach a brand's declared territory without leaking third-party identity, and can stay restrained on a conservative brand. A person did that authoring, reading the same sources the model will read.

Step 3 tests something narrower and harder: whether synthesis, following written instructions, produces a grammar as viable as the one that person wrote. The fixtures are the standard. The bet of ADR 0016 rests here, because a grammar that only works when a human writes it is a consulting deliverable rather than a product feature.

**The standard is as viable, not identical.** Two competent authors reading the same sources will choose different entries, different counts, and different words. A synthesized places section that names a different room than the fixture does is not a failure. A synthesized places section that names no room, or names a room the sources do not support, is.

## Stated limitations, named before judgment

**The fixture author saw the same sources the model sees.** This is deliberate and it is what makes parity a fair test of instruction quality rather than a test of who had better information. It also means parity does not establish that the grammar is correct, only that instructions can reproduce what a careful reader produced from the same material. If the fixtures are wrong, a passing synthesis reproduces the error.

**The fixture author knew the gates.** The step 1 fixtures were written by someone who had read the ADR and knew what the gates would test. The instructions now encode much of that knowledge, which is the point, but it means the comparison is generous to the instructions in a way a fresh brand would not be.

**The drewOn asymmetry carries over from step 1.** The Mycopop product record carries an image and the Dialog Health record does not. Any clause reading grounding from provenance metadata is affected; the clauses below cite entry content instead.

**Dialog Health's brain predates the ADR 0015 basis field.** Its Lived World environments carry earned prose and no basis object, so synthesis has less to inherit than on Mycopop. Expect thinner derivation on that client and do not read it as an instruction failure without checking the source material first.

**One model call per client per run.** Synthesis is not sampled three times the way step 1 sampled suggestion sets, because each run costs a full brain synthesis. Set-to-set variance is therefore unmeasured, and a single run cannot distinguish an instruction failure from a bad draw. A clause that fails on one run and passes on a rerun is recorded as unstable rather than as passed.

**Grammar content is the only thing under test.** These runs also regenerate guidance sections, the dossier, the Lived World, and the story architecture. Changes there are out of scope for this gate, and are recorded as findings if they look like regressions.

## Per-section parity clauses

Applied to each of the six sections, on each client, comparing the synthesized section against the same section of the hand-authored fixture.

### Clause 1: Territory reached

**Pass:** the synthesized section covers the same physical ground the fixture section covers, whether or not it uses the same specifics. For Mycopop's objects section, the fixture reaches a period-hardware world through original forms; a pass reaches that world too, by whatever specifics it chooses. For Dialog Health's objects section, the fixture reaches phones, console views, forms, and ordinary clinic objects; a pass reaches those.

**Fail:** the section misses ground the fixture covers and the sources support, or it reaches ground the sources do not support.

### Clause 2: Honesty held

**Pass:** entry counts track what the sources actually document. Specifically, Dialog Health's light section stays at or below three entries, because that brand's identity guidance states photography is undocumented; and no section on either client is padded to its maximum.

**Fail:** a section is filled beyond what its sources support. The diagnostic is per entry: an entry whose derivedFrom cannot be checked against a real source, or whose derivedFrom names a source that does not say what the entry claims, is invented.

This clause is the over-prescription gate from step 1, relocated to synthesis. It is the clause most likely to fail, and a failure here is the most important result this evaluation can produce.

### Clause 3: Origins correct

**Pass:** every entry's origin is defensible against the instruction's rule. An entry resting on a source with provenance ours and aspiration current is evidence or inference; an entry resting on any other combination is ambition. Mycopop carries at least one ambition entry, because it has two direction sources at intake. Dialog Health carries zero, because it has none.

**Fail:** any ambition on Dialog Health. Any Mycopop entry deriving from the direction sources that is labeled evidence or inference. Any entry labeled ambition whose derivedFrom points at the brand's own current material.

**Dialog Health carrying zero ambition entries is the sharpest single signal in this gate.** It is the one place where the instruction can be checked against a brand that supplies no direction sources at all, so a stray ambition there means the rule did not take.

### Clause 4: Derivations real

**Pass:** every derivedFrom names a source or artifact field that exists, and the named thing supports the statement. Checked by sampling: three entries per section per client, chosen before reading them, traced back to the brain payload and the source register.

**Fail:** any sampled derivedFrom that names something that does not exist, or that does not support the entry it justifies.

### Clause 5: Section-specific tests

- **Places:** entries name rooms, surfaces, or materials, not journey moments. **Fail:** any place entry that is a restatement of a Lived World environment name without a physical space written for it.
- **Camera:** meets the mechanical settings check above and reaches at least three entries, per the schema floor. **Fail:** fewer than three, or any entry that is a mood statement in settings clothing.
- **Rejects:** every entry names something visible. **Fail:** any reject that names a claim, a tone, a promise, or a business outcome rather than something a camera could record.
- **People:** casting only. **Fail:** any persona or audience language, judged by the mechanical check.

---

## Overall gate

**Pass:** both clients pass every mechanical check, and each of the six sections on each client passes clauses 1 through 5.

**Partial:** the mechanical checks pass and one or more section clauses fail. Recorded per section with the failing entries quoted. A partial result is a real result and is reported as one; the recommendation then states whether the failures are instruction defects, source defects, or fixture defects.

**Fail:** any mechanical check fails, or clause 3 fails on either client.

If the gate does not pass, that is a successful evaluation with a negative result. It gets recorded plainly. The instructions are not iterated silently until they pass; any revision is a new pre-registered run recorded beside this one, with the original result left standing.

## Limitation amendment, recorded rather than judged around

The pre-registered limitations state that the fixture author saw the same sources the model sees, and that this is what makes parity a fair test of instruction quality. **That premise is partly false and the correction belongs here rather than in a quiet adjustment to the judgment.**

Template and product-brief sources never reach synthesis. The fixture author read them. On Dialog Health this matters concretely: two fixture rejects rest partly on `RCS Template.png`, which synthesis never saw. On Mycopop the filtered sources are two product briefs, whose content reaches production through the product records instead, so the effect is smaller.

The amendment does not excuse every gap. Where a fixture entry rests on a source that synthesis did receive, its absence is still an instruction result. That distinction is applied per entry below.

A second limitation, unforeseen: several Mycopop entries derive from the Instagram screenshot, an image. Their derivations state what the image shows, and **this evaluation cannot verify image-derived claims**. Entries citing the screenshot are judged on internal consistency and on agreement with the approved brain's own record of that source, not on independent verification. This is the step 1 drewOn asymmetry arriving in a new form.

## Findings

**Finding 1: a prohibition can be labeled as an aspiration in the reader.** Two Mycopop rejects carry `origin: "ambition"`, because they derive from the 8-bit and Odyssey sources and the instruction's rule assigns ambition by the source's provenance and aspiration. The rule is applied correctly. The display is wrong: `basisNote` renders an ambition entry as "A direction you're reaching for", so the reader will show "No copied game worlds" under a label that says the brand is reaching for it.

This is an edge the ambition rule did not anticipate and that neither fixture exposed, because both fixtures assigned their IP rejects to evidence by hand without noticing that was a judgment.

**Resolved 2026-08-16 by owner ruling: rejects always carry evidence or inference, never ambition.** Origin answers whether a statement is a fact about the brand or a declared aim, and a reject is neither: it is a rule, in force today even when the material that motivated it is aspirational. The direction source is still recorded in `derivedFrom`. Written into the cycle 2 instructions; no display change was needed, because the label was correct and the origin was wrong.

**Finding 2: rejects have no stated source of their own.** Diagnosed above. The fix is one paragraph in the instructions naming the guardrails and the approved prohibitions as the first place to look for the rejects section, and it is small enough to be a single revision.

**Finding 3: the template and product-brief filter is invisible to the person building a brain.** Sources marked as templates or product briefs never reach synthesis. That is correct behavior and it is documented nowhere the user can see. A person who uploads a background template as brand material will not learn that the Brand Brain never read it. Belongs in the deferred register rather than this ADR.

### What this cannot be called

**It cannot be called a regression caused by the revision, and it will not be called one here.**

| --- | --- |
| Stimulant-culture signaling (Mycopop) | Guardrail `Do not imitate stimulant culture`, and `livedWorld.rejects`: aggressive gym-bro or extreme-energy signaling |

**The cycle 2 instruction names guardrails and stated prohibitions as the source for rejects. It names neither `livedWorld.rejects` nor the guidance sections.** Two of the three unreached territories rest primarily on `livedWorld.rejects`. The third rests on a creative guidance principle. None of the three has its primary grounding in the place the instruction sends synthesis to look.

**Reasoned:** the unreached territories are explained by instruction scope rather than by guardrail thinning, and no model call was needed to establish it. The thinning hypothesis is not disproved, because the fresh guardrails are uncaptured, but it is no longer the leading explanation.

## Why this matters more than it looks

ADR 0016 part 3 retires `livedWorld.rejects` from the image path once grammar rejects compile, per client, at re-synthesis. If grammar rejects do not pick up what `livedWorld.rejects` was holding, that transition does not move those refusals from one channel to another. It drops them.

On the evidence here that is exactly what would happen to two refusals: aggressive gym-bro and extreme-energy signaling on Mycopop, and fear-based depictions of patients on Dialog Health. Both are currently compiling as avoid-clauses through the live path. Both are absent from both cycles of grammar rejects on their client.

**This is a step 4 blocker rather than a step 3 defect**, and it is recorded here because step 3 is where it became visible. It is not fixed in this commit: the owner ruled that nothing changes in the instructions while the stability check runs, and the fix is a one-line widening of the rejects-source block to name `livedWorld.rejects` and the creative guidance principles alongside the guardrails. It should be made deliberately, after the stability check, so its effect can be seen against a measured baseline rather than against a single draw.

# Stability check, pre-registered

Written and pushed before any stability run exists. Commit order is the proof, same discipline as both prior cycles.

## What it measures and why

Cycle 2's people section fell on both clients and the cycle 2 judgment declined to call it a regression, because one run per client cannot separate an instruction failure from a bad draw. This check answers that question and nothing else. **The instructions do not change while it runs.**

Three runs per client on the unchanged cycle 2 instructions. **The cycle 2 capture counts as a fourth data point**, since it was produced by the same instructions, giving four runs per client rather than three.

## Clauses and pass conditions

Defined before any output exists. All counts are entries per section.

**Clause S1, people count stability.** Across the four runs on cycle 2 instructions, take the range of the people entry count, meaning highest minus lowest.
- Range of 0 or 1: **stable**.
- Range of 2 or more: **variable**.

**Clause S2, people coverage.** A coverage marker is defined per client, before reading output.
- Dialog Health: at least one entry that casts a message recipient, a patient or an employee receiving communication, as distinct from staff sending or monitoring it. The brand's approved world guidance requires both sides.
- Mycopop: at least one entry that constrains who may appear with the product, of which the cycle 1 adult casting default is one instance and not the only acceptable one.

Count how many of the four runs carry the marker.
- 0 of 4: **stable and thin**. The instructions have a real gap and a targeted revision is justified.
- 4 of 4: **stable and adequate**. Cycle 2 was misread and no revision is warranted.
- 1, 2, or 3 of 4: **variable**. The instruction is not the cause and no instruction change is justified on this evidence.

**Clause S3, other sections.** The same range test as S1, applied to all six sections on both clients. Every section with a range of 2 or more is reported as variable. This clause has no pass or fail; it produces the variance profile that S1 and S2 are read against, and it exists so a section that moves is caught whether or not anyone was watching it.

**Clause S4, rejects durability.** Across the four runs: `ambition` in rejects must be zero in every run, on both clients. Any occurrence fails the clause and reopens the resolved finding 1. The rejects entry count range is reported under S3 rather than judged.

**Clause S5, mechanical durability.** Every mechanical check from the parity rubric runs on every stability capture. A single failure in any run fails the clause, because the mechanical rules are absolutes rather than tendencies.

**Clause S6, the guardrail question.** The stability captures include the regenerated dossier guardrails and `livedWorld.rejects`. For each run, record whether each of the three unreached territories appears in the regenerated guardrails, in the regenerated `livedWorld.rejects`, in the grammar rejects, or nowhere. This closes the question the existing captures could not answer.

## Decision rule, stated before the runs

- People **stable and thin**: a targeted instruction revision is justified, scoped to the people section and pre-registered as its own cycle.
- People **variable**: no instruction change. The finding becomes a property of the system and goes to step 4.
- Any other section **variable** under S3: recorded, not acted on, for the same reason.
- S4 or S5 failing: that outranks everything above and is reported first.

## Stated limitations

Four runs is enough to distinguish stable from variable at the coarse level these clauses use, and not enough to estimate a distribution. Temperature and other model parameters are whatever the synthesis path sets; this check does not vary them. All runs use `mode: "initial"`, so each regenerates the whole brain, and section counts elsewhere in the artifact may move for reasons this check does not isolate.

## Notes for step 4

**The source-filtering premise is permanent, not a judging artifact.** This evaluation corrected its own limitation once it found that template and product-brief sources never reach synthesis. That correction was written to keep cycle 1 honest, but the underlying fact does not expire with the evaluation. Grammar rejects grounded in a brand kit are only ever as good as what reaches synthesis, and the decision about which sources are filtered sits upstream of grammar quality permanently.

Step 4 inherits this as an input rather than as a caveat. When the grammar begins compiling into the image path and grammar rejects replace `livedWorld.rejects` as the avoid-clause source, the strength of a brand's refusals will be a function of a filter chosen for a different reason, in a different part of the system, with no visibility to the person who uploaded the material. A client whose identity assets arrived as a template will have weaker protection in its compiled prompts than a client whose arrived as approved guidance, and nothing in the interface will say so.

Do not treat this as solved by a better instruction. The instruction can only reach what synthesis is given.

**Whatever the stability check finds is a step 4 input either way.** If sections vary meaningfully across runs at this temperature, step 4's consumption design inherits that as a property of the system rather than as a defect someone forgot to fix: a compiler reading a grammar section cannot assume the section it read last week is the section it reads today, and a re-synthesis changes the compiled prompt in ways nobody asked for. The guidance given to clients about when to re-synthesize inherits it too, because re-synthesis stops being a neutral refresh and becomes a redraw of the brand's visual direction. If instead the sections are stable, that is equally a step 4 input, and a more comfortable one: it means a grammar can be treated as a fixed artifact between approvals.

**The `livedWorld.rejects` transition is a step 4 blocker.** Recorded in full above. Grammar rejects are scheduled to replace `livedWorld.rejects` in the image path per client at re-synthesis, and on the evidence of both cycles that transition would drop at least two refusals that are compiling today. Step 4 must not ship that switch until grammar rejects demonstrably carry what the old channel carried.
